# The Phone Layout

How tmuxy lays itself out on a phone, upright and held sideways, and what a
finger does there. The state is the `phone` slice of the app machine and the
size actor ([STATE-MANAGEMENT-ACTORS.md](STATE-MANAGEMENT-ACTORS.md)); the
E2E suites are `tests/12-touch*.test.js` ([TESTS-E2E.md](TESTS-E2E.md)).

```

| panes (80 x 24)          |     | menu   | panes (80 x 24)| side   |
| tmux status line         |     | tree   | status line    | pane   |
| keys 6 x 2               |     |        |                |        |
+--------------------------+     +--------+----------------+--------+
          ====  bar              | keys 12 x 1                       |
```

## Layout

On a phone (`(pointer: coarse), (max-width: 600px)`, or the `phoneLayout` config override a story uses) the size actor sends `SET_PHONE_LAYOUT` and the machine fixes every `SET_TARGET_SIZE` at 80 × 24: the window is never sized to the screen. Instead the cell shrinks until 80 columns span the pane container exactly (`infra/dom/phoneLayout.ts`), which on a phone has 4px of padding each side so the grid's outer borders stay on screen; `SET_CHAR_SIZE` carries the grid's own font size, which `App.tsx` scopes to the terminal so the chrome keeps its size. The layout is then, with no tab strip (the tree lists the tabs, a slide on the terminal switches them), the terminal with tmux's line under it in the chrome's type (the host at the left edge, the session at the right, a mode's chip alone in a mode), the key row (`components/phone/KeyRow.tsx`: two rows of six, edge to edge — esc, tab, ctrl and alt, whose latches go through `infra/dom/keyLatch.ts` for the next key from the row or the phone keyboard; the prefix key, which presses the bindings' prefix as one chord and is lit while the prefix is armed, a second tap cancelling it; the ⌨ key, the one thing that puts the phone's keyboard up or away; then the arrows, which repeat while held and hand over to the arrow a finger slides onto (`components/phone/keyRepeat.ts`), enter, and the mic, which listens until the speaker pauses and types what it heard into the pane as the phone keyboard would, through the browser's speech recognition (`infra/dictation.ts`); a second tap stops it early, and a microphone the site may not use, a missing one or a browser with no recogniser says so in a notice), a bar a finger drags (`components/phone/PhoneDivider.tsx`, rules in `domain/phoneSplit.ts`: down zooms the terminal, its status line with it, as one picture from the top-left corner as far as the bottom view gives room; up from its own size folds the terminal and the keys away; down again brings them back; the zoom and the fold are the `phone` slice's `phoneZoom` and `phoneCollapsed`, kept per device in local storage (`infra/prefs.ts`), and a double tap moves the bar with the zoom it sets), and the bottom view below it (`components/phone/PhoneBottomView.tsx`): three views — the app menu as a list whose submenus are pages (`components/phone/PhoneMenu.tsx`), the tree, in front at first, and the side pane (the dock, `components/phone/PhoneDock.tsx`) — picked by a tab bar along its top or a sideways swipe, a slide like the one between tabs, settling by the tab slide's commit rule (`components/phone/bottomSlide.ts`). The dock is the desktop's pinned right-column terminal, the same `sidebar-right` window, created the same way on first use and sized to the view it is drawn in (`SET_PHONE_DOCK_SIZE` writes `@tmuxy-sidebar-cols`/`-rows`); upright it sits on the bottom view's grey, as the tree does, and held sideways on the window's, as the desktop's column does. Held sideways (`SET_PHONE_LAYOUT`'s `landscape`, read from the app body's keyboard-free size rather than `(orientation: landscape)`, which a keyboard on a portrait phone flips), the layout is flat, as a desktop's: the terminal in the middle with the cell cut from the height so its 24 rows, the status line and the keys' one row of twelve along the bottom fit (`phoneLandscapeCell`), the tree on its left under the menu's button, which puts the app menu in the tree's place (`components/phone/PhoneLeftSide.tsx`), and the side pane on its right, started for the column and drawn as the desktop's (no header, the window's background); each column keeps at least `PHONE_SIDE_MIN`, the cell cut to the width when the height would leave less.

## Touch

What one finger does on the terminal is `components/terminal/usePaneTouch.ts` over the pure `components/phone/touchGesture.ts`: a tap focuses the pane, and an open keyboard follows it, while a tap on the pane that already has the keyboard puts the keyboard up or away, as the key row's ⌨ does (a double tap undoes its first tap's toggle); a double tap zooms the terminal in the browser to twice its size around the point tapped — the `phone` slice's `phoneZoom`, the bar's zoom too, drawn as a `transform` on the picture (the pane container and the status line) inside a scrolling viewport, so tmux and the cell measurement see nothing, and a finger then pans it; a hold selects the word under the finger in copy mode (`COPY_MODE_WORD_SELECT`, kept as a `pendingWordSelection` until the row loads) and dragging on extends it, with Copy and Cancel over the pane (`components/terminal/TouchSelectionBar.tsx`); a mostly-sideways slide is the desktop's two-finger tab slide (`GESTURE_SWIPE`, the same `gestures` slice). The dock's pane is drawn by the phone too but is not the tab grid, so a slide on it switches nothing. The page is installable as a standalone app (`public/manifest.webmanifest`, the icons beside it and the meta tags in `index.html`); installed, `standalone.css` keeps the phone layout clear of the status bar and home indicator, and held sideways of the notch. A phone's browser tints its own bars from the page: `theme-color` follows the theme's window colour (`infra/themeManager.ts`), and in a browser the body and the phone's fixed root paint that colour opaque, which is what Safari reads (a translucent one read as white). Installed, the status bar sits above the page (`default`, not `black-translucent`: under a status bar drawn over the page iOS 26 blurs the top edge whatever covers it); the phone's fixed, painted root is the box at that edge, and an opaque band covers the home indicator (`standalone.css`); an app installed under the old status bar still gets its missing height back (`standaloneShortfall` in `components/phone/usePhoneViewport.ts`). The status line under the terminal is the desktop's — the hints at the left, the host beside the session at the right — in the same small type, 80% of the terminal's font at the phone's cell, on a row just tall enough for it, so the line written for 80 columns fits the screen's. A phone's keyboard covers the page rather than resizing it, so the app follows the visual viewport (`components/phone/usePhoneViewport.ts`): the bottom view gives way first (while the dock has the keyboard, the terminal folds away instead, the keys staying), then the terminal is cut short and scrolled to keep the cursor's row in view; the type never shrinks for a keyboard. A phone on a session shared with a desktop client shrinks that client's window to 80 × 24 too, as any smaller client does.
